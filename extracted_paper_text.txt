crashes [1, 2]. Driver drowsiness induces prolonged reaction latencies, degraded situational awareness, erratic steering inputs, and involuntary microEmail addresses: roshan23bcs9@iiitkottayam.ac.in (Roshan Binoj),

Existing technologies for driver state estimation broadly fall into three

and computer vision-based behavioral monitoring [2]. Intrusive physiologi-

non-intrusively, but suffers from severe lag; driving anomalies manifest only

Despite substantial advances in deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs),
35

bottlenecks in real-world deployment:
1. Environmental and Lighting Sensitivity: Dynamic cabin environments exhibit extreme illumination fluctuations, including direct
sunlight glare, sudden shadow casts, tunnel transitions, and near-zero

40

nighttime ambient lighting, which frequently compromise single-RGB
frame appearance features [5, 6].
2. Facial Occlusions and Individual Variance: Drivers frequently
wear prescription glasses, tinted sunglasses, or face masks, and display
diverse facial morphology, which degrades holistic spatial CNN repre-

45

sentations [7, 8].
3. Temporal Ambiguity and False Alarms: Drowsiness is intrinsically a multi-timescale temporal process. Standard frame-level classifiers and recurrent networks (RNNs/LSTMs) often fail to reliably
distinguish between rapid voluntary blinks (100–250 ms) and involun-

50

tary fatigue-induced micro-sleep episodes (> 400 ms), or between natural conversation and genuine yawning, causing disruptive false-positive
alarms that lead drivers to disable ADAS features [9, 10].
4. Absence of Contextual Vehicle Safety Risk Quantification:
Contemporary models exclusively output static categorical labels (e.g.,

55

“Alert” vs. “Drowsy”) without quantifying the continuous temporal evo3

lution of fatigue or coupling it with real-time vehicular dynamics (such
as vehicle velocity and forward Time-to-Collision), thereby failing to
trigger actionable, progressive ADAS interventions [3, 1].
To decisively overcome these challenges, this paper proposes the Spatial60

Geometric Temporal Transformer (SG-TTrans), a novel dual-stream
deep spatio-temporal framework coupled with a dynamic Risk-Aware Safety
Index (RSI). The proposed system architecture concurrently processes raw
appearance video streams through a lightweight convolutional backbone and
extracts 3D facial landmark kinematics (computing Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR),

65

Mouth Aspect Ratio (M AR), Head Pose Euler angles, and PERCLOS). A
multi-head temporal self-attention transformer integrates these spatial and
geometric modalities across temporal windows, utilizing a mathematically
proven Temporal Dynamic Decay Attention (TDDA) mechanism to filter
high-frequency transient blinks and amplify prolonged drowsiness signatures.

70

The network simultaneously outputs fine-grained driver fatigue states and
computes a continuous RSI metric that enables graduated autonomous vehicle safety actions.

2.1. Recent Literature Survey (2024–2026)
Hassan et al. [5] presented a hybrid framework combining Swin Transformer backbones with diffusion-based de-noising models to address extreme
illumination shifts and nighttime camera noise. Their model attained an

110

accuracy of 96.2% on simulated datasets; however, the iterative sampling
of diffusion components incurs high computational overhead (> 65 ms latency), limiting real-time edge deployment. Al-Nafjan et al. [8] developed
“DrowsyDetectNet”, a lightweight compact CNN optimized for edge devices
that detects eye closure and yawning with 94.8% accuracy, yet its single-frame

115

spatial analysis remains susceptible to false alarms from voluntary blinks.
Zhang et al. [11] designed a Spatio-Temporal Vision Transformer for
combined driver fatigue and distraction detection using cross-modal visual
attention, reporting 96.5% accuracy on NTHU-DDD, though performance
degrades under eyeglasses occlusion. Kumar et al. [9] proposed a facial land-

120

micro-sleep detection, achieving 95.8% accuracy on UTA-RLDD; however,
their architecture relies solely on landmark coordinates without preserving

6

an accuracy of 96.9% at the expense of higher feature extraction latency.
Chen et al. [10] introduced a dynamic Temporal Convolutional Network
(TCN) operating on multi-scale facial regions of interest, reaching 95.3%
accuracy with low memory footprint, but showing vulnerability to sudden

130

3D head pose rotations. Liu et al. [13] proposed a cross-attentional fusion
network integrating 2D facial landmarks and 3D head pose dynamics, achieving 96.1% accuracy on YawDD, yet lacking explicit mechanisms to suppress
transient voluntary blinks. Rahman et al. [14] investigated “ViT-Driver”,
incorporating dynamic token pruning into a Vision Transformer for edge de-

135

95.1% accuracy, though with reduced sensitivity to subtle micro-expressions.
Zhao et al. [1] developed an intelligent safety framework modeling driver
fatigue state transitions through hidden Markov models, linking driver drowsiness to driving risk categories; however, their vision frontend relied on basic

140

CNN features without temporal self-attention. Patel et al. [4] introduced
a deep spatio-temporal graph convolutional network (ST-GCN) tracking facial landmark topologies and gaze vectors for multi-task alertness estimation,
reporting 96.7% accuracy on private automotive datasets. Silva et al. [6] proposed a PERCLOS-enhanced Temporal Vision Transformer tailored for low-

The overall pipeline of SG-TTrans operates sequentially across four core
stages, as illustrated in the system architecture diagram in Figure 1:
1. Multi-Modal Input & Dual-Stream Feature Extraction: An in-

200

cabin camera captures video frames at 30 FPS over a sliding temporal
window of length T . The Spatial Appearance Stream extracts dense
feature tokens ztvis ∈ Rdv using a lightweight MobileNetV4 backbone.
Concurrently, the Geometric Kinematics Stream detects 68 facial landmarks via MediaPipe FaceMesh, computing Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR),

205

Mouth Aspect Ratio (M AR), PERCLOS, and 3D Head Pose Euler
angles to construct a kinematic token ztgeom ∈ Rdg .
2. Cross-Modal Fusion & Positional Tokenization: The spatial and
geometric tokens are linearly projected, concatenated, and injected
with learnable 1D temporal positional embeddings Epos to form the

210

input sequence Z0 ∈ RT ×dmodel .

4. Multi-Task Output & Dynamic Risk Assessment: The pooled
sequence representation is mapped through dual prediction heads: (a)
